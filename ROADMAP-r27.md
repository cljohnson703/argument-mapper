## Approved, not yet built

* **"A car ... it" as one claim about some car.** "Mary bought a car, and she loves it" is read as one claim the check does not take apart, its "it" tied to "a car" (r27.211), so "so Mary bought a car" does not follow. Read it as what it says: there is a car that Mary bought and that Mary loves. Then "Mary bought a car" follows by dropping one part under "some", and so does "Mary loves a car". So "Mary bought a car and loves it". The user, 2026-11-14: "a" (on the roadmap, after the "possibly" work). Meanwhile, written "Mary bought a car, and Mary loves the car", it follows by conjunction elimination.
* **Davidson's trick, two more steps.** Existential introduction on objects ("Bob loves Mary; so Bob loves someone"), and dropping a place or instrument phrase ("Bob ran in the park; so Bob ran"; "Bob buttered the toast with a knife; so Bob buttered the toast"). The user: "So long as it continues to respect the limits of Davidson's trick." Not after "almost", "allegedly", "hardly", "fake" or "former", nothing under "not", and no idioms ("fell in love", "ran into Mary").
