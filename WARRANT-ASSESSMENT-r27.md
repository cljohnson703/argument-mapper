# Warrant assessment

The final contention pill now has two labels: Warranted and Unwarranted. Its explanation retains the distinction between absent support, surviving opposition, weak challenges and conflicting arguments. Unwarranted does not mean false. The assessment uses the argument actually supplied on the map.

The same conservative assessment applies at every level, including weak-on-weak exchanges. An argument is active when its inference is certified and its premises survive; defeated when a required premise is refuted or a counterexample establishes invalidity; otherwise pending. Pending objections and rebuttals, strong or weak, leave their targets unresolved and cannot simply cancel one another. A genuinely defeated challenge no longer blocks surviving support. This is a map-assessment policy, not a classical first-order inference. A certified support whose premises fail remains unable to warrant its conclusion.

An explicitly parsed warrant claim—such as denying that two quoted premises establish a conclusion—can itself be Warranted when the corresponding argument has a grounded undercut. An unsettled challenge to that undercut does not automatically establish the warrant claim. A parsing failure or circular dependency is not positive evidence for it either. Only the precisely matched quoted argument is treated this way; other pending challenges to the warrant claim still block it.

Unrecognized and ambiguous inferences require clarification. A bounded counterexample search can establish invalidity for explicit propositional formulas using up to eight distinct letters. Quantified, modal and warrant expressions remain outside that search. Failure to find a counterexample neither certifies an inference nor bypasses the one-rule-per-step requirement.

Rule validity is unchanged: modus ponens can remain valid while a required premise is weakly challenged. Internal refuted/unresolved/unestablished states remain distinct for propagation and explanations.

A successful strong rebuttal inside a contention's opposition can also supply support for that contention when its own surviving premises certify the exact contention in a single inference. The verdict records that proof and explains it as support supplied by the rebuttal. Merely attacking an objection's required premise does not qualify. Weak rebuttals, unrecognized or multiple-step shortcuts, unattached arguments, missing references, and circular uses of the contention do not supply such support. Other surviving or pending challenges still block warrant. This uses the original premise assessments and works for reference groups as well as canvas children. `indirect-warrant-r27-test.js` tests the distinction, including evaluation-order permutations.

Qualified non-establishment claims (including parsed “X is not ruled out”) also receive map-relative warrant when every strong argument against them has a grounded undercut. This requires a refuted or successfully weakly challenged premise, or a certified chain whose alternative supports are all undercut; an explicit counterexample can also defeat an attempted establishment. Unknown steps, cycles, missing premises, surviving independent attacks, and pending weak challenges do not meet this requirement. The result concerns the arguments presented, not every possible argument or the truth of X. Ordinary affirmations and negations receive no such warrant. First-order parsing and inference validity are unchanged.

`qualified-undercut-r27-test.js` preserves the complete user-supplied Zombie Argument Map as a regression, including its reference group. Both the referenced and unreferenced versions warrant the qualified main contention. The reference now carries established map-relative grounds from the functionalism branch instead of bypassing those grounds by treating a repeated premise as bare.

Cross-reference spacing removes the text's extra 5px bottom margin on referenced nodes, in both reading and editing mode. The centered badge is also raised a further 1.5px to account for the last line's font leading.

Validation includes unsupported and conflicting maps, 126 strong/weak chains through six levels, nested English weak challenges, supplied support with weak versus strong defenses, quoted warrant claims, invalid versus unknown inferences, invariant modus ponens, label rendering and SVG exports.
